Golden Rod Stakes Picks: Tiger Baby Fires Back for Kenny McPeek

A debut winner who ran the fastest number in the field. Another unbeaten filly who won going this exact trip. And the only stakes winner in the race, who some may overlook.

By Tom Woodruff · Nov 23, 2026

The Golden Rod is a Grade 2 for 2-year-old fillies at 1 1/16 miles on the Churchill Downs dirt, worth $400,000. Eight are entered, and this is one of the last big two-turn tests of the year for juvenile fillies.

It's a young, lightly raced field. Two of our top three have run only once, and only one filly in the race has won a stakes. That makes this a race about upside, not proven class.

Here's how I see it. The filly who ran fastest last time is the one to beat, but there are a few others with strong reasons to improve going two turns.

#7Tiger Baby

Record1: 1-0-0Earnings$69,088Last startWon at Churchill, Nov 20SirePlainsman

Tiger Baby tops our ratings. She won her debut at Churchill Downs on November 20, going a mile against 10 rivals, and that race gave her the best recent figure in the field. She's by Plainsman and bred to handle the longer trip.

Kenny McPeek trains, and he has a strong record with second-time starters. She also worked sharply before her debut. E. Esquivel rides.

The questions are experience and the quick turnaround: this is only her second start, and it comes eight days after her first. But on raw ability, she's the one they all have to catch.

#4Breezy

Record1: 1-0-0Earnings$63,938Last startWon at Keeneland, Oct 17SireGirvin

Breezy is also one-for-one. She won her debut at Keeneland on October 17 going 1 1/16 miles, the same distance as this race, and she did it at 10-1 against nine others.

She's by Girvin and bred to go long. William Walden trains, and he has a strong record with route runners. F. Prat rides, so these are top-flight connections. She sits just off the pace and should get a good trip.

#6Stand by Me

Record2: 1-1-0Earnings$93,088Last startWon at Churchill, Oct 25SireBy My Standards

Stand by Me took a big step forward to break her maiden at Churchill Downs on October 25, beating 11 others at 6 furlongs. In her debut here in September she was 2nd, beaten a little over three lengths.

This is her first try around two turns, but she's by By My Standards and bred to handle it. Eric Foster trains and T. Gaffalione rides.

#8Wharfie

Record3: 2-0-0Earnings$180,740Last startWon at Churchill, Oct 25SireEssential Quality

Wharfie is the only stakes winner in the field. She won the Listed Rags to Riches here on October 25 at 1 1/16 miles, the same track and trip as Saturday. She has won two of three starts and leads the field with $180,740 in earnings.

William Mott trains, and he has a strong record with last-out winners. She's by Essential Quality and bred for this. J. Alvarado rides. She ranks a bit lower on our numbers, but she's proven at the trip, and that counts for a lot in a field this green.

#2Dazzling

Record5: 1-1-0Earnings$149,923Last start2nd at Keeneland, Oct 9SireTapit

Dazzling may be the one who goes to the front. She's a Tapit filly trained by Steven Asmussen, with Jose Ortiz riding, and she was 2nd in an allowance at Keeneland in October, beaten a length.

All four of her recent starts came on turf, though, including a maiden win at Kentucky Downs. This is a test of whether she handles dirt.

#1Spectacular Sam

Record4: 0-3-0Earnings$66,695Last start2nd at Keeneland, Oct 7SireCyberknife

Spectacular Sam is still a maiden after four starts, but she has the second-best recent figures in the field and three 2nds, including one in a stakes at Monmouth. She was 2nd at Keeneland at 1 1/16 miles last time, beaten two and a quarter lengths.

L. Saez rides for trainer Robert Medina. She could easily fill out the exotics.

Race shapeHow the Race Sets Up

There's no confirmed front-runner here, so Dazzling may be the one sent to the front. Tulsi can also be close early, and most of the rest like to sit just off the pace.

Without much speed, this could turn into a tactical race where position matters. Fillies who sit close and finish, like Tiger Baby, Breezy and Wharfie, should be in the right spot.

The bottom line
